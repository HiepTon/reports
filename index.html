Summary

Analysis

Organizations should review third-party access controls and IAM policies, specifically looking for evidence of unauthorized access to S3 buckets or similar cloud storage.

Summary

Researcher HD Moore conducted an experiment by planting 2 million 'canary' secrets into AI prompts, revealing widespread data leakage across the AI supply chain. The findings show that AI routers and inference resellers frequently log prompts containing sensitive credentials, and in some cases, attackers have compromised providers to siphon this data. The study also identified instances where providers ignored 'Zero Data Retention' privacy flags and scraped user data for their own purposes.

Analysis

This research confirms that the AI supply chain is currently a major vector for credential theft and privacy violations. Organizations must treat AI prompts as untrusted input and strictly prohibit the inclusion of API keys, passwords, or sensitive PII in any prompts sent to third-party AI services. Security teams should assume that any data sent to an AI provider is being logged and potentially exposed.
