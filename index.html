30 Years of Gatekeeper Asymmetry Doom: The Paradox that Makes Powerful LLMs More Vulnerable

Summary

Check Point Research explores the 'Gatekeeper Asymmetry Doom' principle, which posits that systems vetting adversarial input are inherently weaker than the systems processing that input, leading to inevitable security failures. The research applies this concept to various security controls, including static analysis and AI guardrails, arguing that current defenses often fail because they cannot match the power of the systems they are meant to protect. They recommend narrowing this gap by upgrading gatekeeper power.

Analysis

Security teams should recognize that relying solely on simple, static gatekeepers (like basic pattern matching or signature-based detection) is insufficient against modern, powerful threats. Defenders need to move toward more robust, context-aware security layers that can better analyze complex inputs, rather than relying on outdated, asymmetric defense models.
